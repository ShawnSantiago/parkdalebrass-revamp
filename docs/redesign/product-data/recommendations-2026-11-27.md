# Facts needed: what I recommend for The Brockton and The Bellwoods

*2026-11-27. These are recommendations for the owner to decide. They are not facts about either piece and must never be copied into product fields. The research rests on search snippets, because page fetching is blocked in the agent's environment. A second agent fact-checked the claims. Regulatory and sizing points that it confirmed are cited. Where it couldn't re-check something, the text says so, and the price comparables in §6 are in that group.*

## 1. Summary

Launch both pieces as plain, unplated brass at an independent maker's price, not a plated fashion price. For The Brockton I suggest **CAD 150**. That sits inside the converted range of US unplated brass signets (about CAD 124–169), just above the old Brockton special edition (CAD 140). For The Bellwoods, no price comparable passed the fact-check, so set its price from landed cost. Use CAD 125 only as a planning figure for supplier quotes. **Decide the finish first** (raw, sealed or plated), then get the alloy and lab reports from the supplier. Those drive cost, care copy, resizing, and which claims and rules apply. Then fix landed cost, then price. Launch in Canada, add the US once lead results meet California's limit, and leave the EU for later.

- The 30 days and the free exchange are judgement. Prepaid labels cost money, so set a budget.
- Ontario's CPA 2002 covers internet agreements over $50. Disclose these terms before purchase and send a copy of the agreement within 15 days. If the disclosure is missing, the buyer can cancel until 7 days after getting the copy. If no copy is sent, the buyer can cancel within 30 days ([CPA 2002](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30/v25), [CLEO](https://www.cleo.on.ca/en/publications/otmshop/how-do-i-cancel-agreement)).

## 5. Compliance checklist

### Ask the supplier for

Ask per SKU, per finish and per part, including chain, clasp, jump rings and solder:

1. Alloy designation and composition, with a mill certificate.
2. How each part is made.
3. Finish or coating: the product name and chemistry. For any plating: metal, karat, thickness in µm, and underlayer metal.
4. Lab reports:
   - lead (ppm) in each part and coating;
   - cadmium (ppm) in metal parts and solder;
   - nickel release to EN 1811:2023, with EN 12472 wear pre-treatment if coated.

   Each report should name the lab, its accreditation, the SKU, finish, batch and date.
5. Country of manufacture, if you'll disclose it.

The list follows from the limits below (judgement). If the supplier has no reports, get 2–3 lab quotes.

### Claims to avoid until documented

- The list in `04-content-and-data.md` §1: handmade, Canadian-made, solid brass, hypoallergenic, nickel-free, lead-free, sustainable, lifetime, tarnish-proof, waterproof, skin-safe. The Competition Act requires an "adequate and proper test" before a performance claim ([s.74.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-34/section-74.01.html)). Treating these words as performance claims is an inference, but a safe one.
- "Gold plated", "gold filled", "G.E.P." or any karat mark. The Canadian plating thickness wasn't resolved, so read the current [Regulations](https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1303/FullText.html) first.
- "Harmless" for green marks. Once a sample confirms it, care copy can say brass may leave a faint green mark that washes off with soap and water ([Jewelers Mutual](https://www.jewelersmutual.com/resources/individuals/cleaning/how-clean-and-care-brass-jewelry), [Nebraska Medicine](https://www.nebraskamed.com/node/22586)).
- The pipe heritage, unless it's in your own wording (D-39).

### How to show prices

- Use round 0 or 5 endings, like 150 or 125. Avoid .99 endings and just-below prices like 149.
  - Round prices "feel right" for purchases driven by feelings ([Wadhwa and Zhang, 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/678484)).
  - Firms use rounder prices to signal quality ([Stiving, 2000](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:46:y:2000:i:12:p:1617-1629)).
  - 99-endings signal a low price or a discount ([Schindler and Kibarian, 2001](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00913367.2001.10673654)).
  - Applying these findings to jewelry is an inference.
- Show one price. Don't show a compare-at price or a launch discount, and never show the pipe prices as a "was" price.
  - A "regular" price must pass the Competition Bureau's volume test or time test, and a new product can't pass either ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/ordinary-selling-price)).
  - Since June 2022, an advertised price must include every mandatory fee except government-imposed charges such as sales tax ([s.74.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-34/section-74.01.html)).
- For the US, set round USD prices by hand if your plan allows it, because automatic conversion won't keep a chosen ending. I haven't checked this in Shopify's documentation.

### If landed cost comes in higher

1. Count everything first: making, finish, packaging, freight, duty, brokerage and payment fees.
2. Keep at least 4x. Change the spec before the multiple: a lighter section, a simpler finish, a plainer chain, fewer ring sizes.
3. Get a second supplier quote.
4. Raise the ring price only to about CAD 165–170, the top of the converted range.
5. If it still doesn't work, delay the piece rather than cut the margin.

## 7. Open questions for you

1. Finish: raw, sealed or plated? The same for both pieces?
2. Who is the supplier? Can they document every part, including chain, clasp and solder? How is each piece made?
3. What are the landed cost per unit, the minimum order per ring size, and the reorder lead time?
4. Can the maker resize or remake a Brockton?
5. Do you have legacy size data, or will the waitlist ask for ring size?
6. Canada only at launch, or the US too? If the US, will you set USD prices by hand?
7. Who pays return shipping on refunds? What's the budget for exchange labels?
8. Will product copy mention the pipes, and in what wording?
9. Will you ever offer engraving?
10. Will a lawyer review the policy against the Ontario CPA?
