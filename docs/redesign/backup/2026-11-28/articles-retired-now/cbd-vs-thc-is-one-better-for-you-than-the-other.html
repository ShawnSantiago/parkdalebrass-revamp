How THC Works & How it Affects Your Brain

The effects of THC on the brain are many and varied. THC binds to cannabinoid receptors in the brain, which are located in the regions that control memory, thinking, concentration, pleasure, and time perception. The binding of these receptors by THC causes a variety of effects.

THC is a natural chemical found in marijuana plants that is responsible for its psychoactive effects. When someone smokes or consumes marijuana, THC passes from the lungs or stomach into the bloodstream and eventually reaches different parts of the brain where it interacts with cells called neurons. There are two main types of neurons: those that release neurotransmitters (chemical messengers) and those that receive them.

Neurons that release neurotransmitters are called presynaptic neurons; neurons that receive neurotransmitters are called postsynaptic neurons. When THC enters the brain, it attaches to molecules on these neurons called cannabinoid receptors which then affect how they work together to produce a wide range of effects including euphoria and increased appetite, two of the most common side effects of smoking weed.
