## What changed from the 2019 text

The 2019 terms are Shopify's 2019 template, unchanged. This draft keeps its broad structure (overview, using the site, products, prices, orders and billing, third-party tools and links, submissions, personal information, errors, prohibited uses, liability, severability, governing law, changes, contact) and rewrites each part. It drops, or replaces with fairer wording:

- the "as is … without … merchantable quality, fitness for a particular purpose, durability" disclaimer, which can't apply to goods sold to consumers (CPA s. 9; ON-17);
- "we reserve the right to refuse service to anyone for any reason" and the open right to cancel or limit any order (ON-18), now a closed list of reasons with a full refund;
- "prices … subject to change without notice" with no protection for orders already placed;
- "continued use … constitutes acceptance" of changes (ON-18, ON-21), now "the version in effect when you order applies";
- "governed by … the laws of Canada" (PROV-02), now Ontario law without limiting the buyer's home-province protection;
- the indemnity clause (a consumer indemnifying the store and its suppliers for legal fees), and "ambiguities … shall not be construed against the drafting party", which contradicts CPA s. 11;
- "the age of majority in your state or province" and the "minor dependents" sentence;
- the US-style "headings for convenience" and "entire agreement … superseding" wording, replaced by a plain list of what the agreement is made of.

Also relevant: Electronic Commerce Act, 2000 s. 21, under which a consumer's material error in a transaction with an automated system may not be enforceable against them if there was no chance to prevent or correct it. 4. **When the agreement is formed, and pricing errors.** The draft says the agreement starts when the order confirmation is sent, because that email is the s. 39 copy and payment may be taken at checkout. Many retailers instead say acceptance happens at shipping, to keep room to cancel for pricing errors. Which do you prefer? The draft avoids the issue for prices by always offering the buyer the choice to pay the correct price or cancel for a full refund, and never charging more without consent. Is that enough under CPA ss. 14, 15 and 17 (no false, misleading or unconscionable representations)? Option consommateurs' report for ISED found that the law outside Quebec is unsettled, but that merchants in Ontario, Alberta and BC can generally rely on the error to refuse delivery. Quebec CPA s. 224(c) bars charging more than the advertised price, and Quebec decisions vary (one 2015 Small Claims decision let a retailer cancel). 6. **Implied conditions and limits on liability.** The 2019 "as is" disclaimer of merchantable quality and fitness is gone, because CPA s. 9 applies the Sale of Goods Act implied conditions (ss. 14 and 15) to consumer goods and voids terms that negate them. 7. **Risk of loss and title.** The draft leaves a placeholder for who bears loss in transit and after the carrier's delivery scan. 8. **Governing law, forum, arbitration and class actions.** The draft names Ontario law but keeps the buyer's home-province consumer protection and their local courts (PROV-02), and has no arbitration clause or class-action waiver.
