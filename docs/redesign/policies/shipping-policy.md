# Shipping policy

This policy explains where Parkdale Brass ships, how long delivery takes, what it costs, and what we do if an order is late, lost or damaged. It applies to orders placed at parkdalebrass.com. Returns, exchanges and refunds are covered in full in our [Returns and exchanges policy](https://parkdalebrass.com/policies/refund-policy).

## Where we ship

We ship to addresses in Canada, in every province and territory except Quebec. We don't ship to Quebec or outside Canada at the moment, and checkout accepts only addresses we ship to.

We deliver to the street address you give at checkout. We don't deliver to P.O. boxes.

## How long it takes

- **Processing:** orders ship 1 to 2 business days after you place them.
- **Delivery:** once your order ships, it arrives in 2 to 7 business days, wherever we ship in Canada.

That's 3 to 9 business days from the day you order.

A business day is Monday to Friday, except Ontario public holidays: New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day and Boxing Day (December 26). An order placed on a weekend or holiday counts from the next business day.

## Shipping costs

- **Orders under $129.00 CAD:** $15.00 CAD, flat rate.
- **Orders of $129.00 CAD or more:** free.

The $129.00 is the total price of the jewelry in your order, before tax and shipping. The same rate applies everywhere we ship.

Checkout shows the shipping charge, the tax and your total before you pay.

## Taxes, duties and currency

Prices on our site are in Canadian dollars (CAD) and don't include tax. Sales tax is added at checkout, and it also applies to the shipping charge.

Your order ships within Canada, so you won't pay customs duties, import taxes or brokerage fees on delivery.

## Carrier and tracking

We ship every order by Canada Post [Canada Post service], with tracking. Parcels travel by [method of transport].

When your order ships, we email you its tracking number.

Orders don't need a signature on delivery. Please choose a delivery address where your parcel can be left safely.

The day Canada Post's tracking shows your parcel as delivered is your delivery date. If the tracking doesn't show a delivery date, it's the day you receive your parcel. Your 30 days to return or exchange a piece, or to report damage, start on your delivery date.

## If your order is late

You can follow your parcel with the tracking number in your shipping email. If you haven't had a shipping email within 2 business days after you ordered, or your order hasn't arrived within 7 business days after it ships, email us at shawns@parkdalebrass.com and we'll look into it.

If your order hasn't been delivered within 30 days after you placed it, you can cancel it at any time before it's delivered. If you agreed in writing to a later delivery date, the 30 days count from that date instead.

To cancel, tell us you're cancelling. You can tell us any way you like. We suggest a way you can keep a record of, such as an email to shawns@parkdalebrass.com. If you write to us, your notice counts from the day you send it. We refund everything you paid, including shipping, within 15 days after you tell us.

If those 30 days pass and you then agree to take delivery, you can't cancel for late delivery, but your return rights still apply.

If your parcel reaches you after you cancel, send it back within 15 days after you cancel or after you receive it, whichever is later. Send it to our address under "Contact us" below, or to a return address we give you, by a method that confirms delivery, such as a tracked service. It counts as returned on the day you send it. We pay the reasonable cost of sending it back: we can send you a prepaid label, or pay you back what you spent.

If we don't refund you in time and you paid by credit card, you can ask your credit card issuer, in writing, to reverse the charge. You need to ask within 60 days after our 15 days end.

## Lost or missing parcels

Your parcel is our responsibility until Canada Post delivers it to your address.

**Lost before delivery.** If Canada Post loses your parcel, we'll send you a replacement or refund everything you paid for the order, including shipping. You choose. If we can't replace it, for example because the piece is out of stock, we refund it. If you choose a refund, we make it within 15 days after you tell us.

We treat a parcel as lost when Canada Post tells us it is. If your order still hasn't arrived 30 days after you placed it, you can also cancel it, as explained above.

**Marked as delivered, but not received.** If tracking shows your parcel as delivered but you can't find it:

1. Check around your door, your mailbox or parcel box, and with neighbours or anyone else at your address.
2. Check the delivery address in your order confirmation.
3. If it's still missing, email us at shawns@parkdalebrass.com with your order number, as soon as you can.

We'll ask Canada Post to look into it, and tell you what it finds. If Canada Post finds the parcel was lost, or was delivered to an address other than the one on your order, we treat it as lost, as above.

## Damaged in transit

If your jewelry arrives damaged, email us at shawns@parkdalebrass.com within 30 days of delivery. Include your order number and photos that show the damage. A photo of the box or mailer helps too, if you still have it.

We'll replace the piece or refund it. You choose. We send you a prepaid label to return the damaged piece, and we ship the replacement to you free. If we can't replace it, for example because it's out of stock, we refund it. Our [Returns and exchanges policy](https://parkdalebrass.com/policies/refund-policy) explains how refunds are made.

This time limit doesn't shorten your rights under the law. The jewelry you buy must match its description and be of merchantable quality.

## Returns and exchanges, in short

- You can return or exchange unworn pieces in their original packaging within 30 days of delivery. Unworn means you've only tried the piece on to check the fit, and it has no marks, scratches or other signs of wear. Original packaging is the packaging the piece came in, not the outer shipping box or mailer.
- Each order with a ring includes one free size exchange within those 30 days. We send you a prepaid return label, and we ship the new size to you free. We don't resize rings: if The Brockton doesn't fit, exchange it for another size.
- If you return an unworn piece for a refund (not an exchange), you pay the return shipping. This doesn't apply when you cancel under a right the law gives you.
- If a piece arrives damaged, has a fault, or isn't what you ordered, tell us within 30 days of delivery, with photos. You choose a replacement or a refund. We send you a prepaid return label, and a replacement ships to you free.
- We refund to your original payment method within 5 business days after your return reaches us. Your bank or card provider may take longer to show the refund.

How to start a return or exchange is explained in our [Returns and exchanges policy](https://parkdalebrass.com/policies/refund-policy).

1. **Delivery disclosure (R7).** *Point:* is a window enough, and must the carrier and the mode of transport appear? *Applied:* the policy states the place of delivery (a street address in Canada outside Quebec, no P.O. boxes), the window (ships 1 to 2 business days after the order, arrives 2 to 7 business days after shipping, so 3 to 9 business days in all) and the carrier (Canada Post). Item 8 asks for "the date or dates" of delivery "as applicable", and a stated window gives them. Item 9 ii requires "the name of the carrier … and … the method of transportation" when the supplier "holds out a specific manner of delivery and intends to charge the consumer for delivery". The store names Canada Post and charges $15.00 under $129.00, so `[Canada Post service]` and `[method of transport]` stay as owner placeholders. The owner's "delivered in 2 to 7" is read as counting from shipping. That reading never promises more than the owner said: if the owner meant 2 to 7 days from the order, customers get their jewelry sooner than the policy says. The order confirmation should also show the estimated delivery dates (store follow-up). *Source:* [O. Reg. 17/05 s. 32, items 8 and 9](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050017) (verified).
2. **Late-delivery cancellation (R13, R14, R15, R17).** *Point:* which section, its trigger, what counts as delivery, the refund, the notice, a parcel arriving after cancelling, and deemed delivery. *Applied:*
   - (a) The right is CPA s. 26. Sections 22 to 26 apply to future performance agreements over the prescribed amount (s. 21(1)), which is $50 (O. Reg. 17/05 s. 23.1). An order not delivered when it's placed is a future performance agreement (s. 1). O. Reg. 17/05 s. 18 switches off only ss. 22, 23 and 44 to 47 for an internet agreement that is also a future performance agreement, so s. 26 applies. The policy offers the right to every buyer.
   - (b) Trigger: s. 26(1) runs 30 days from "the delivery date specified in the agreement", s. 26(2) from the order date "if the delivery date … is not specified". A window isn't plainly a specified date, so the more protective s. 26(2) reading is applied: "within 30 days after you placed it". It never gives less time than "30 days after the latest delivery date we gave you", which it replaces. A later date the buyer agreed to in writing restarts the 30 days (s. 26(1)(a)).
   - (c) Delivery means the parcel reaching the buyer's address. Section 26(4) deems delivery only for attempts at the consumer (refused, or no one there after reasonable notice), and the policy says the parcel is the store's responsibility until delivered (point 3). So handing it to Canada Post isn't delivery for this right.
   - (d) Refund: "any payment made under the agreement" (CPA s. 96(1)(a)), within 15 days after the day the consumer gives notice (O. Reg. 17/05 s. 79(1)). So the policy says "everything you paid, including shipping, within 15 days after you tell us".
   - (e) Notice: it "may be expressed in any way", "may be oral or in writing and may be given by any means", and written notice is given when sent (CPA s. 92(2) to (4); s. 94). So the policy says "in any way you like", and email is only a suggestion.
   - (f) Parcel arriving after cancelling: return it to the supplier's address within 15 days after the later of the notice and receipt, "by any method that provides the consumer with confirmation of delivery". It counts as returned when sent, and the supplier pays the reasonable cost (O. Reg. 17/05 s. 81(1) items 1 and 3, (2) to (4)). There's no "unused" condition. The supplier's address is the one set out in the agreement (s. 78), so the policy points to "Contact us".
   - (g) The deemed-delivery cases (s. 26(4)) aren't listed. With no signature needed they rarely arise, and listing them could read as narrowing the right.
   - Waiver: s. 26(3) is kept as "If those 30 days pass and you then agree to take delivery …".
   - Other provinces: their harmonized internet-sales rules weren't read (ISED's template site isn't among this session's sources), so the policy cites no provincial rule. It gives every buyer the Ontario right, which applies whenever the supplier is in Ontario (CPA s. 2(1)).

   *Source:* [CPA 2002 ss. 1, 2(1), 21, 26, 92, 94, 96](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30) (verified); [O. Reg. 17/05 ss. 18, 23.1, 78, 79, 81](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050017) (verified); Internet Sales Contract Harmonization Template (not verified, not relied on).
3. **Risk of loss: lost in transit, and lost after a "delivered" scan (R21).** *Point:* who bears each loss, and which date starts the 30-day windows. *Applied:* by default, delivery to a carrier is delivery to the buyer "in the absence of evidence to the contrary" (SGA s. 31(1)), property passes on that appropriation (s. 19, Rule 5(ii)), and risk passes with property "unless otherwise agreed" (s. 21). The policy agrees otherwise: "Your parcel is our responsibility until Canada Post delivers it to your address." That costs nothing beyond the law in practice, because an undelivered order can be cancelled for a full refund under CPA s. 26 (point 2). Lost parcels are replaced or refunded in full, including shipping, and the buyer chooses: "replace or refund" with no chooser is ambiguous, and CPA s. 11 reads it for the consumer. A refund follows the 15-day rule of point 2. A parcel counts as lost when Canada Post says so, with the 30-day cancellation as the backstop. For "delivered but not received", the policy gives first steps, the store asks Canada Post to investigate, and a parcel found lost or delivered to another address is treated as lost. The text never says the buyer bears the loss. That would be a representation about rights and remedies, which CPA s. 14(2) item 13 bars if it's misleading. The 30-day windows start on the delivery date in Canada Post's tracking, since every order is tracked (D-57). *Source:* [Sale of Goods Act ss. 19, 21, 31](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90s01) (verified); [CPA 2002 ss. 11, 14, 26](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30) (verified).
